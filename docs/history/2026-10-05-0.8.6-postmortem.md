# Postmortem: 0.8.6, a ~100-line fix that took a working day

2026-10-05. Written by the comms tech lead at Steven's request, for every team lead on this host.

## What was asked, and what it cost

Steven asked for `herdr-aify env` to stop killing its workers: closing or leaving the env window should
detach, a second launch should join the running instance, and each `--stop` should end only its own instance.

- The fix was written and committed at **03:52** (aify-wrapper `388cd55`).
- The final reviewed source landed at **10:54** (aify-comms `62a3b06e`), seven hours later.
- At 13:00 it was still not tagged, held behind a test gate on the final commit.
- In that window: 6 wrapper commits, 11 comms commits, 4 review rounds, 11 full wrapper-suite runs (plus 2 partial
  reruns and 3 isolated reruns), 6 full comms three-suite cycles, 4 env-suite runs.
- The cost that matters: **0.9, the actual overnight goal, did not start.** Its developer spent the night reviewing
  0.8.6.

What 0.8.6 delivers to Steven, in full:

1. `herdr-aify env` detaches, joins, and stops by mode (the requested fix);
2. `hermes-aify` stops printing the gateway bearer token to its log (a real find from review);
3. a spawn left in `starting` is failed by the reconciler instead of hanging forever (an incident fix I added).

None of it is deployed: teams are working, so nothing can restart.

## Root causes

1. **The roadmap was serialised behind a patch release.** I planned the 0.9 role swap for "after 0.8.6 ships", so
   the developer who should have built 0.9 reviewed 0.8.6 instead. Nothing forced that order; 0.9 does not depend on
   0.8.6.
2. **Review had no stopping rule.** Every finding was fixed, and every fix opened another round. Most findings were
   races a reviewer can construct (pid reuse, a lock reclaimed between two reads, a sleep that overshoots its
   deadline). One finding in four rounds, the token print, touched what Steven actually runs.
3. **Scope crept into an open release.** A new incident arrived mid-review and I added its fixes to 0.8.6. Each
   addition bought at least one more full round.
4. **The gate was a lottery on this host.** The release rule was "every suite fully green on the final commit".
   Under shared load (host commit charge 108-128 GB of 132 GB during the runs) the wrapper suite failed a different
   set of real-process and render tests each run, and each passed alone. Of the 10 completed full runs, 3 were green
   (all on earlier commits). The others failed 1, 1, 4, 7, 10, 29 and 63 tests. The final commit changed three
   lines of one shell template, plus its test. Its three full runs failed 10, 1 and 7 tests, and every one of those tests passed alone.
   A gate that goes green 3 times in 10 measures the host's load, not the code. I kept rerunning instead of
   asking for a call.
5. **Every round paid a full verification cycle.** Each round reran all five suites across three repos, about
   1-1.5 h per round on this host, even when the change touched one file in one repo.
6. **The test stack is dominated by a few slow tests.** Measured on the 12:13 wrapper run (723 tests, 4,405
   test-seconds; it runs in parallel, so wall time was 38 min):

   | tests | share of tests | test-seconds | share of time |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 124 slowest (each 10 s or more; the worst 60-140 s) | 17% | 3,937 | **89%** |
   | 510 fastest (each under 1 s) | 71% | 28 | 0.6% |

   The slow ones render a launcher through `install.sh` and start real processes. They are also the ones that time
   out under load, so they cause both the cost and the unreliability. How many of them prove the same property
   twice has not been measured yet; that is the first question of the test-stack cleanup.

## What every team can take from this

- **Give a small fix a budget, and say when it is blown.** Name the time up front ("one hour"). Past it, tell
  Steven what is left and what it is worth, before continuing.
- **Triage review findings by what the operator runs.** Fix what hits a real path. File constructed edge cases in
  KNOWN_ISSUES unless the fix is cheap and contained. A round that only closes hypotheticals ends the review.
- **Keep a release closed.** A new incident gets its own slot, even when the code is right next door.
- **Do not let the roadmap wait on a patch.** If the next version does not depend on the patch, run them in
  parallel.
- **Make the gate achievable on the host you actually have.** Measure your suite's wall time and failure rate
  under real load. If it cannot go green, ask for the gate call early. Rerunning for hours is not evidence.
- **Verify at the right size.** Per round, run what the change touches plus its cross-repo checks; run everything
  once, at the end.
- **Measure where your suite's time goes.** If about a sixth of the tests carry about nine tenths of the time,
  those tests are the work. Fewer, faster tests with the same coverage beat a larger suite nobody can run.
- **The host is shared.** One team's long suites and test agents slow every other team's gates. Announce heavy runs.

## What changes in aify-comms

- 0.9 now goes to comms-senior-dev (developer); the comms tech lead reviews. First slice G2a, sent 2026-10-05.
- Review and verification follow the rules above. Recorded in the tech lead's working rules and in the 0.9 handoff.
- Test-stack cleanup: the wrapper suite's 124 slow tests come first. Measure what each proves, merge or delete the
